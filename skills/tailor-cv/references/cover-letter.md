# Cover-letter guide

`preferences.md` can override anything here except the truth rule.

## Shape

- One page: letterhead (same as the CV), date, addressee, salutation, three or four short
  paragraphs, sign-off.
- **Opening:** why this role at this employer, tied to something specific in the posting.
  No throat-clearing.
- **Evidence:** the one or two most relevant concrete achievements. Tell the story behind a
  CV bullet instead of repeating it.
- **Fit:** connect the user's skills or domain to the employer's product, team, or problem.
- **Close:** one or two sentences inviting a conversation.

## Facts

- Same truth rule as the CV: nothing that isn't in the master CV or confirmed by the user.
  That includes small embellishments (a problem "slowing down", an outcome "customers
  noticed") the source doesn't state.
- **Date:** get today's date from the system (`date +%Y-%m-%d`) and write it the way the
  document language normally does: `September 15, 2026`, `15 September 2026`,
  `15. September 2026`, `15 Eylül 2026`. Never guess the date.
- **Addressee:** use the hiring manager's name only if the posting gives it. Otherwise
  address the team or employer in the document language's usual way.
- **Location and logistics:** if the role is somewhere the user isn't, don't state
  relocation or availability as fact. Ask the user, or add a clearly marked line they can
  remove, and mention it in your report.

## Sound like a person

- No em dashes (—) anywhere in the letter, including its `<title>`, which becomes the PDF's
  title. Use commas, periods, or colons, and "to" for ranges.
- Write the way the user would talk to a hiring manager: warm, direct, specific.
  Contractions are fine where the language uses them.
- Avoid template phrases: "I am writing to express my interest", "I am excited to apply",
  "I believe I would be a great fit", "passionate", "leverage", "synergy", "dynamic team",
  "proven track record".
- One concrete detail beats three adjectives.
- Vary sentence length, and don't start every paragraph with "I".
- Match the formality the document language expects, such as formal address in German or
  Turkish letters.
